Core finding: a transnational repertoire of locally rooted protests, not a proven unified global organization. Common grievances and portable symbols recur, while local triggers, groups, state responses and political outcomes differ. Counterpoint: Carnegie tracker analysis suggests youth-led protest rates in 2017–2019 may have been comparable or higher than 2025.

Cases: Bangladesh, Kenya, Serbia, Indonesia, Timor-Leste, Philippines, Nepal, Madagascar, Morocco, Peru, Bulgaria, Mexico and parallel youth/student mobilizations in India, France and Albania. Do not assume shared command. Do not attribute Peru's broader political crisis solely to Gen Z. In India, distinguish reported voter-list deletions from contested allegations of partisan intent.

Limitations: purposive qualitative sample, not a world census; no original interviews, field work, experiments, surveys, comprehensive social scraping or original world protest dataset; not peer-reviewed publication; fast-moving claims after snapshot date need fresh verification. Provisional quality score 9.2/10 due those remaining evidence gaps, not a claim to have reached 9.8.
