WHO OWNS
THE future?

From tax bills and school corridors to social-media bans and water shortages, young people are taking their anger into the street. But this is not one revolution with one command centre. It is a set of local fights that increasingly recognize themselves in one another.

The counterpoint matters

Is this really a
new global wave?

Not in the simple sense. The connections are real, but the novelty claim is much less secure.

WHAT THE PATTERN SUPPORTS

A transnational repertoire is forming.

Movements have borrowed symbols, tactics and digital spaces across borders. The One Piece flag is one portable cultural reference. [S27]

WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE

One command or shared ideology.

Carnegie's analysis suggests youth-led protest rates in 2017–19 were comparable to or higher than in 2025. Similarity does not prove one organization. [S3]

The working model

Four mechanisms interact, often at different speeds.

Local shock

A thinking tool, not a universal law. Movements can be contained early, split or produce outcomes no organizer intended.
