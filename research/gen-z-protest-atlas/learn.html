FIELD NOTE 01
GLOBAL · 2024—26
From zero to analytical fluency

LEARN TO READ
A PROTEST.

Build the vocabulary, then practise separating what happened from what people say it means.

Trigger

The event that makes people act now: a bill, ban, death, service failure or scandal.

Root grievance

The older condition that makes a trigger feel intolerable.

Frame

The explanation connecting individual frustrations to a common public problem.

Mobilization

Turning concern into coordinated action and physical assembly.

Repression

Coercive attempts to stop protest, including force, intimidation or communications restrictions.

Institutional translation

Converting pressure into enforceable decisions and accountability.

Expert habit

Ask what would
prove a claim wrong.

Strong analysis names evidence that would challenge the story, not only evidence that supports it.

WEAK CLAIM

“Social media caused the revolution.”

This confuses an organizing tool with why people risk taking part. It ignores offline ties and political context.

STRONGER CLAIM

“Digital networks lowered coordination costs in a crisis.”

Test it against timelines, interviews, platform evidence, offline organizing and cases where similar tools did not produce mass protest.

Glossary

Words worth knowing.

Use these while reading the atlas and paper.

Decentralized
Coordination distributed among groups rather than controlled by one formal centre. Leaders can still exist.
Diffusion
Spread of tactics, symbols or ideas. A copied flag proves borrowing more readily than shared strategy.
Collective action
People acting together around a shared problem, even if they disagree elsewhere.
Political opportunity
Features of the environment that make action easier or harder, such as elections, elite splits and legal space.
Backfire
When repression creates sympathy or recruits participants. A possibility, not a law.
Frame alignment
How a message connects individual frustration to shared injustice and collective response.
Institutionalization
Turning temporary mobilization into durable rules, organizations, representation or accountability.
Source-attributed estimate
A number linked to its producer, method, time window and counting categories.
Purposive sample
A chosen set of cases. It can reveal mechanisms without representing the whole world.
Counterfactual
A structured “what if” comparison used to test causal explanations.
Practise like a researcher

Three small projects.

All can be done with public sources and a spreadsheet. Protect individuals when working with protest evidence.

Starter · Timeline audit

Build a ten-row timeline with date, event, source URL and confidence. Separate the trigger from later escalation.

Intermediate · Outcome tracker

Record if each demand was reversed, adopted, implemented, investigated or dropped. Re-check after three months.

Advanced · Diffusion test

Find dated evidence of a tactic moving between movements. Do not infer coordination from visual resemblance alone.

A useful routine Start with an authoritative source, follow its citations, search for evidence that challenges it, record what is established, then separate your interpretation.
