MECHANISM 01

Focal-event effect

An immediate event makes abstract grievances concrete, but does not explain every part of a movement.

Limit: a trigger explains timing more readily than readiness, scale or duration.

A causal checklist

What to test in each case.

Use these to avoid explanations that sound neat but ignore contrary evidence.

  1. Why this moment? Identify the focal event, then list existing grievances.
  2. Why these participants? Examine campus networks, workers, civic groups, rural access and absent voices.
  3. Why did the message travel? Separate the platform, the message and evidence that people acted.
  4. What did the state do? Track concessions, bans, arrests, force, surveillance and counter-messaging.
  5. What changed afterward? Verify laws, leaders, elections, prosecutions, budgets and reversals separately.
